# Disturbances, growth, and oscillation

A perturbation is a small change from a background state, such as a tiny ripple in electric potential. Linear stability analysis asks what happens while that ripple is small enough to evolve independently of other ripples. Plasma density and temperature gradients can supply energy to particular disturbances.

A simple example is $A(t)=A_0 e^{\gamma t}$, where $A_0$ is the starting amplitude, $t$ is time, and $\gamma$ is the growth rate. A positive rate means the disturbance grows, a negative rate means it decays, and a rate of zero means its amplitude stays constant for that mode. The real angular frequency $\omega$ measures how fast the phase of the ripple changes. A ripple can grow while it oscillates. For example, the reference deuterium run reports $\gamma=4.172$ and $\omega=0.946$ in GENE units.

A fast-growing fluctuation can eventually participate in turbulence. The linear calculation ends before nonlinear interactions and saturation determine the transport of particles and heat. It is one step toward understanding confinement.
