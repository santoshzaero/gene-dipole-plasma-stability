# Compensated density-gradient pilot and low-wavenumber controls

## Three kinetic species at fixed deuterium gradient

In the original equilibrium, electrons and singly charged deuterons have equal density profiles. Differentiating charge balance enforces equal density gradients. Adding fully ionized helium gives a local equilibrium path with \(n_e=n_D+2n_{\rm He}\) and \(\mathrm{omn}_e=(1-f)\mathrm{omn}_D+f\mathrm{omn}_{\rm He}\), where \(f=2n_{\rm He}/n_e\) at the reference surface.

A positive local profile family can be constructed by taking \(n_D(x)=n_{D0}\exp[-2(x-x_0)/L_{\rm ref}]\), \(n_{\rm He}(x)=n_{{\rm He}0}\exp[-\mathrm{omn}_{\rm He}(x-x_0)/L_{\rm ref}]\), and defining the electron profile by the charge sum. Its reference logarithmic gradient is the one supplied in GENE. The helium fraction is fixed **across runs at the reference surface** and can vary across flux surfaces within each model. Reference temperatures are equal, with a common temperature gradient of 2. The model is an electrostatic local equilibrium in the prescribed magnetic dipole. It does not reconstruct global force balance or particle sources.

| Species | GENE charge | Mass in proton units | Density in electron units | Reference temperature | `omt` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Electron | −1 | 0.00054462 | 1.000 | 1 | 2 |
| Deuterium | +1 | 2 | 0.750 | 1 | 2 |
| Fully ionized helium | +2 | 4 | 0.125 | 1 | 2 |

The input gives `n_spec = 3` with all species active, no dilution species, and `comp_type = 'IV'`. At the reference surface these densities imply `f = 0.25` and effective charge `Zeff = 1.25`. GENE 3.1 patch 5 documents arbitrary kinetic species count and electron-normalized `dens` in `doc/gene.tex`. The local `prof_type = 0` path accepts the prescribed local density and temperature gradients. `src/profiles.F90` checks both charge-weighted density and gradient sums and retains the default gradient guard. `src/collisions_common.F90` and the Landau implementation support multiple active species. The pilot used the same Landau/Xu–Rosenbluth settings and `coll = 0.01` for all three cases. No upstream changes were needed. [GENE source verification points](../references.md)

### Results

Fixed `kymin = 0.7`, `24 × 48 × 24`, velocity extents `lv = 3.5`, `lw = 12.25`, original ring geometry, automatic timestep and `omn_D = 2`. The effective timestep was 0.001087322674 in all three cases. The output namelists reproduce the intended input checks and both charge sums vanish. GENE converged without rescaling in each case.

Both rates increase over this narrow compensated path. The final complex overlaps with the matched midpoint are 0.99999922 and 0.99999934, supporting a similar dominant observed mode. A derivative along this path combines the electron-gradient and helium-gradient responses. Below `omn_e = 1.5` the same path would require a negative helium density gradient, which would correspond to a locally hollow helium profile requiring independent physical justification. Very large gradients would likewise need an ordering and equilibrium check. The midpoint is a three-species baseline with modified composition and collision/polarization terms relative to the two-species reference. A complementary deuterium-gradient pilot could set `omn_e = 2` and pair `omn_D = 1.75, 2, 2.25` with `omn_He = 2.75, 2, 1.25`. No such runs or helium-fraction variation were launched. ## Audit of the original `kymin = 0.3` record

The original kinetic two-species case used the same common gradients and `32 × 48 × 32` grid as the conservative `kymin = 0.5` comparison. It applied a time step of 0.000835 for 3129 reported steps and reached normalized time 2.61355. The native stopping flag and omega row report 378.519 and +220.647. The log has 29 amplitude rescalings, consistent with 29 sharp downward jumps in the binary potential norm. Rescaling keeps floating-point arithmetic in range and does not itself imply numerical failure or a physical change in energy. Here it occurs alongside a rapidly growing signal varying strongly from node to node. The dimensionless adjacent-node norm relative to the field norm is 1.55644, versus 0.00464 at `kymin = 0.5`. The final complex overlap with 0.5 is 0.00156. This magnitude of shape difference would also be compatible with a distinct branch without the numerical evidence. The terminal energy summary printed asterisks for the electron and summed frequency columns, consistent with numbers that exceeded the diagnostic output field width. The log completed normally and recorded no fatal overflow or underflow stop. Its startup warnings include a dipole reference-surface reset to `x0 = 0.707107`, missing `trpeps` for reporting normalized collisionality, the fallback memory estimate, and the rough automatic collision bound. The fixed controls share the geometry reset, missing `trpeps`, and fallback memory warning.

GENE monitors the principal-log phase increment every ten steps. Refitting unwrapped phase with one output per integration step on the final uninterrupted interval gives approximately −2036.823. Using the native spatial weights and unwrapping on the exact final ten-step window yields −2036.785283 before wrapping. Adding three intervals of `2π/(10 Δt_hat)` gives +220.646564. Independently reapplying the native amplitude-weighted complex log ratio gives **378.519047 and +220.646564** from the saved raw field. The reported native frequency aliases the rapidly rotating discrete signal. The few local rate checks match that signal, with no basis for interpreting the large rate as a physical mode. The automatic estimate selected the RKC2 collision restriction at 0.000835, using a reported rough collision estimate of 9258. The corrected controls keep the collision operator and use the specified bound of 9300, giving an RKC2 limit of 0.000831 and an actual step set by the smaller Vlasov step. The completed 32-point run retains two recorded collision calls per time step, as in the original. The original automatic estimator and the fixed-step controls differ in timestep setup, with the collision operator and collision coefficient held fixed.

Both fixed-step runs converged. Their shared late-time fits (normalized time 1.6587275–2.1584750) give growth 4.40346654 and 4.39803863, and real frequency +0.41478279 and +0.41469193, respectively. Relative to the 32-point control, the differences are **0.123%** and **0.022%**. The final complex profiles overlap by 0.999999996 after alignment and interpolation onto the same parallel coordinate. Overlaps with the qualified 0.5 case are about 0.99993. The 32-point control reduces the adjacent-node roughness from 1.55644 to 0.00224, and the 48-point control has roughness 0.00149. Both corrected modes amplify exponentially without the original resets.

These observations support a timestep-sensitive numerical runaway and an aliased real frequency in the original extreme result. A genuine moderately growing IV mode remains at 0.3 after the numerical correction. The exact term that gave the numerical runaway was not isolated. The half-step controls keep the RKC2 collision evaluations per step and use an explicit conservative bound. The corrected 32-point sample is used in the wavenumber figure at common gradient 2 and has slightly higher growth than the 0.5 sample. The 0.1 and 0.2 results and any global growth maximum remain unresolved. No neighboring points or new velocity-grid runs were needed to decide whether the original extreme result survived. [Controlled diagnostics](../../reports/dipole_stability/figures/16_low_wavenumber_controls.pdf)

One startup attempt omitted the collision stability bound and stopped before integration. The corrected fixed-step inputs use `calc_dt = F`, `dt_vlasov = 0.0004175`, and `ev_coll = 9300`, the documented fixed-step collision bound used in prior controls. This setting does not select an eigenvalue solve. Both fixed-step controls retain the original collisional physics, perpendicular wavenumber, velocity grid and extents. The cap increased from 5000 to 10000 to allow the same normalized time span at half step. The second control changes only `nz0` from 32 to 48. Effective parameters and timesteps are confirmed from generated output and binary timestamps. The three pilot points and both controls are strictly IV.

## Provenance and reproducibility

The report's follow-up CSVs record native rates, independently fitted amplitude and phase slopes, normalized field and energy histories, final complex profiles, input/output audit fields, and SHA-256 hashes. The archival inputs and outputs remain intact. Each new case was launched in its own directory with one MPI rank and `MACHINE=mac_arm64 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 /absolute/path/to/gene_mac_arm64 > run.log 2> run.stderr`. Run `python3 analyze_followup.py --runs /absolute/path/to/gene-complete/runs` from the report directory to reread the saved data, followed by `python3 make_figures.py` and `latexmk -pdf -outdir=build dipole_stability.tex`. The extraction script reads raw outputs only. It uses SciPy linear regression on the log of the projected amplitude and on unwrapped temporal phase, and NumPy for field projection, norms and overlaps. The figures and paper also build from their committed data snapshot.
